Robots, software and digital memory can be reprogrammed after they are built. Materials, by contrast, have mechanical behaviour that is fixed at manufacture. This PhD will investigate whether that behaviour can be reprogrammed after fabrication.

You will study architected entangled materials: networks of curved filaments whose stiffness and toughness arise from how the filaments interweave and contact one another. Fabrics and ropes are familiar examples. In these networks, small changes in contact and friction can produce very large changes in mechanical response.

The project will exploit this sensitivity by driving a weak deformation mode, orthogonal to the deformations caused by external loads, to reprogram the network. Ordinary loading does not activate this mode, so the network remains stiff and tough in service. Driving the mode deliberately changes the contact state and releases the network.

Potential applications include spacecraft trusses that can be reconfigured after launch, and wearable braces that stiffen to protect a joint and soften to allow movement.

Key objectives:

- Understand how contact and friction govern stiffness and toughness in each state
- Establish how driving a deformation mode that external loads do not activate changes the contact state
- Determine the range of loading over which this mode remains inactive
- Identify what limits the contrast in stiffness between the two states

The project is approximately 50% experimental, 40% computational and 10% theoretical.

You will join the AIM Lab, a new group in the Department of Aeronautics. As one of its first members, you will help shape the group.

You will develop expertise in the mechanics of slender structures, contact and friction, and the geometry of filament networks. You will fabricate specimens using multi-material 3D printing and a robotic fabrication cell, test them mechanically, and write simulation code to model them.

You will present at international conferences, including the annual meeting of the Society of Engineering Science and the Functionality through Nonlinearity conference. Imperial's Early Career Researcher Institute provides training in research and career skills.
