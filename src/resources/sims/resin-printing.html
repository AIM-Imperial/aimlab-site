AE203 EP1: Manufacturing

Resin 3D printing

Printers such as those made by Formlabs build the part upside down. A build platform dips into a shallow tank of liquid resin. Light from below, through the clear film at the bottom of the tank, turns one thin layer of resin solid. The platform then rises by one layer, and the next layer is cured. Drag the view to turn it.

Light source

A laser traces each layer spot by spot, as in the Formlabs Form 3. A screen shows the whole layer at once and the light behind it cures it in one exposure, as in the Formlabs Form 4 and the Elegoo Saturn printers used for the nose-cone patterns.

What to notice

  • Each new layer is cured against the film at the bottom of the tank, so the platform rises by one layer after each one. In the book's Fig. 20.8 the light comes from above instead, and the platform is lowered into the vat.
  • The laser takes longer for a larger layer, because it must trace the whole area. The screen cures a whole layer in one exposure, so every layer takes the same time.
  • After each layer the platform lifts the part off the film, and liquid resin flows back underneath.
  • The sides of the part are stepped, because it is made of layers. Real layers are 0.025 to 0.1 mm thick, so the steps are much finer than drawn here.
  • The part is the nose-cone pattern. It hangs from the platform by its rod. Afterwards it is washed in alcohol, its supports are removed, and it is cured in an oven.

Schematic. The layers are drawn 3 mm thick, and the part has 23 layers instead of about a thousand. After Kalpakjian and Schmid, Section 20.4 and Fig. 20.8 (pp. 608–610), which describe stereolithography with the light from above, mask projection of a whole layer, and the continuous process with the light from below (Fig. 20.9).
