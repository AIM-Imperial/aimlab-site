Linked knot structures

An edge shared by m faces carries m strands. An integer twist t on the edge passes each strand on to the face t places further round, and the strands follow the faces' corners from edge to edge.

Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, right-drag to pan, F to fit. Edges have length 1. The faces round an edge are taken in right-handed order about it, and a positive twist winds right-handedly. On one edge alone the strands form gcd(m, t) cycles. On a polyhedron every edge has two faces: an odd twist crosses the two strands, an even one keeps each face's own loop, so twists of 2 everywhere give chainmail and odd twists on a spanning tree of the faces give a single strand. For the grid and the honeycombs the strands are counted on one repeating module: a strand that returns to its start shifted by a lattice vector runs on forever with that period, and one that returns unshifted closes into a ring. Each strand runs beside its edge at the offset r, on the side of its face, and turns round the face's corner along a curve tangent to both edges' strands within the corner zone. Strands are coloured either by strand of the module, one colour each while the palette lasts, or so that the curves on show along each edge all differ. The grid and the cubic honeycomb are drawn as closed blocks of cells; the truncated octahedra as whole cells at the corners and body centres of a block. Construction after Yildiz, Önal, Martin, Krishnamurthy and Akleman, ACM Trans. Graph. 45, 184 (2026).
