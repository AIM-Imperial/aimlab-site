AE203 EP1: Manufacturing

Tube extrusion with a spider die

The ram pushes the hot billet through a conical die. A spider holds a mandrel in the middle of the die. The metal flows round the spider's legs, welds back together, and leaves as a tube. Drag the view to turn it.

What to notice

  • The ram moves slowly, but the tube leaves about 10 times faster. The tube's cross-section is about a tenth of the billet's, and the volume of metal does not change.
  • The squares in the billet are squeezed together in the cone and stretched into long, thin strips in the tube.
  • The metal divides round the legs of the spider and joins again behind them, under high pressure, before it leaves the die. Each join leaves a weld line along the tube.
  • Welding inside the die works well only for aluminium and some of its alloys, and no lubricant can be used (Kalpakjian and Schmid, p. 423).
  • At the end of the stroke a short piece of billet, the butt, is left in the container and is cut off.
