Woven lattice geometry

Each beam of a lattice becomes a bundle of helical fibres, and the fibres pass from beam to beam across the nodes.

Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, right-drag to pan, F to fit. Lengths are relative to the unit cell, L = 60. Each beam becomes an n-helix: n fibres wound at radius Reff about the beam axis, where n is the number of beams next to it on the node sphere. The turns per beam nrev step by 1/n; the actual twist is adjusted so that each fibre meets a tangent point of the neighbouring beam. At a node, each fibre follows a great-circle arc on the node sphere from one beam to the next. The rod network is the original lattice, drawn as lines or as rods of the chosen radius. Node spheres are drawn at half the radius of the node sphere. Geometry after Carton, Surjadi, Aymon, Xu and Portela, Nat. Commun. 17, 1581 (2026).
